CASE FILE CR-13

A Discovery at the Clinic

At 12.45 p.m., Secretary Ang went to deliver documents and found Dr Tan dead in his consultation room. A medicine bottle, scattered tablets and a loose cord were recovered nearby. The clinic contacted the emergency services and police immediately.

Dr Tan normally took prescribed medication. Several staff members had handled his medicine bottle as part of their duties, so investigators needed more than fingerprints to establish what happened.

Your task: Identify who deliberately interfered with Dr Tan’s room before the fatal incident, where the interference happened and the evidence that establishes it.

Investigation rules

This is a fictional, non-graphic case. Exactly one of the three suspects deliberately altered the room between 12.14 and 12.21 p.m., acting alone. No accomplices, remote actions or other access routes were involved. Confirmed recordings, access records and scene reports are accurate.

The investigation established that the room interference caused Dr Tan’s fatal fall. The puzzle does not establish a specific drug effect or who administered a substance. Do not infer a poisoning method from scattered tablets.

For the grid, assign each suspect a different location at 12.18 p.m. and associated item. Each location and item must be used once.

Timeline & Evidence

Exhibit 01 · 12.14 p.m. · Room confirmed safe

An authorised staff member inspected Dr Tan’s empty room at 12.14 p.m. A timestamped inspection recording showed the doorway and floor clear, with no obstruction. The staff member then left and secured the room.

Exhibit 02 · 12.15–12.30 p.m. · Corridor camera offline

The corridor CCTV was under scheduled maintenance. This explains a recording gap, not a culprit. Independent room-access records and other cameras remained active.

Exhibit 03 · 12.17–12.20 p.m. · The only entry

A verified access record and entrance-terminal recording identified Dr Lim personally entering Dr Tan’s room at 12.17 p.m. and leaving at 12.20 p.m. He was the only person to enter after the safe-room check and before Dr Tan returned at 12.21 p.m.

Exhibit 04 · The access record’s limits

The room had one usable entrance. The access system recorded every opening. No one used another person’s card, no one followed another person through, and there was no unrecorded entry.

Exhibit 05 · 12.18 p.m. · Mary

Continuous reception footage showed Mary working with the appointment register from 12.14 to 12.22 p.m. She did not leave reception during that interval.

Exhibit 06 · 12.18 p.m. · Secretary Ang

Continuous records-office footage showed Secretary Ang sorting a document folder from 12.14 to 12.22 p.m. She remained there and did not enter Dr Tan’s room.

Exhibit 07 · 12.21 p.m. · Dr Tan returns

The entrance-terminal record showed Dr Tan returning alone at 12.21 p.m. He had not entered during the earlier interference interval. No suspect entered afterwards before the discovery.

Exhibit 08 · The confirmed scene finding

Investigators established that a cord had been deliberately positioned as an obstruction after the 12.14 p.m. check and before Dr Tan returned. The medical and scene findings linked the resulting fall to his death. The puzzle supplies this as a confirmed finding without depicting or describing how to construct it.

Exhibit 09 · Fingerprints on the bottle

Overlapping fingerprints from normal staff handling were found on Dr Tan’s medicine bottle. They could not establish who last handled it, when it was handled or whether anyone tampered with the medicine.

Exhibit 10 · The loose tablet fragment

A tablet fragment was recovered in the corridor near Dr Lim’s room. Its source and time of deposition could not be established. It does not by itself prove that Dr Lim administered anything.

Exhibit 11 · The item records

Dr Lim used the room access card. Mary worked with the appointment register and Secretary Ang carried the document folder. These are the three items assigned in the grid.

Exhibit 12 · A false denial

Dr Lim denied entering Dr Tan’s room, but the independent entry record disproved that claim. His denial supports further scrutiny; the decisive identification comes from the safe-room check, sole entry and interference interval.

Teacher Case Solution

Dr Lim · Dr Tan’s room · Sole entry during the confirmed interference interval

The room was recorded clear at 12.14 p.m. The deliberate obstruction was introduced before Dr Tan returned at 12.21 p.m. Dr Lim personally entered from 12.17 to 12.20 p.m. and was the only possible entrant during the interval. Mary and Secretary Ang were continuously recorded elsewhere. Under the stated single-actor and complete-access rules, Dr Lim uniquely caused the room interference.

The supplied confirmed scene and medical finding links the obstruction to the fatal fall. The puzzle does not establish who administered any substance or that medication prevented movement. Overlapping prints on a routinely handled bottle and an undated tablet fragment cannot establish those claims.

The maintenance-room visits happened earlier and cannot override the later safe-room inspection. Resentment and a false denial are not substitutes for the decisive access evidence.

Ending: investigators submit the verified room-interference findings to the appropriate authorities. The medical examination and any medicine-related questions are considered separately.
